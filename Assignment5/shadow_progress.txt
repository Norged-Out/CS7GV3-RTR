shadow progress

1. what ShadowMap.* does

The ShadowMap class is basically the manager for my current shadow system.

Right now it:
- creates the shadow framebuffer
- creates the depth texture for the shadow map
- sets up the light-space matrix for a directional light
- begins the shadow pass
- ends the shadow pass
- lets the main scene shader sample the shadow texture later

So in short, ShadowMap is the thing that owns the shadow-map resources and handles the light view/projection setup.


2. what the shaders do

shadow.vert:
- sends geometry into light space during the shadow pass

shadow.frag:
- does basically nothing right now
- the GPU just writes depth automatically into the depth texture

scene.vert:
- sends world position, normal, UVs, tangent basis, and light-space position to the fragment shader

scene.frag:
- does the actual lighting
- samples textures
- handles optional normal mapping
- samples the shadow map
- does either hard shadows or PCF shadows


3. how I did baseline shadow maps

For the baseline version, I render the scene twice.

First pass:
- render from the light's point of view
- store only depth in the shadow map

Second pass:
- render the scene normally from the camera
- convert each fragment into light space
- compare its depth against the depth stored in the shadow map

If the fragment is farther than the stored depth, it is in shadow.
If not, it is lit.

That is my current hard-shadow baseline.


4. how I did PCF shadow maps

After the baseline worked, I added PCF.

Instead of checking the shadow map once, I sample a small 3x3 area around the current shadow-map coordinate.
Then I average those results.

So:
- hard shadows = one compare
- PCF shadows = a bunch of nearby compares averaged together

This makes the edges softer and less jagged.

Important thing I learned:
- PCF helps soften edges
- PCF does not really fix shadow acne
- bias handling is what matters more for that
